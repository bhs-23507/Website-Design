Agriculture

Historically, agriculture has been a cornerstone of Ghana’s economy. It accounts for about 19-20% of GDP and employs a significant portion of the population, especially in rural areas. Cocoa is Ghana’s most important cash crop, making the country one of the world's leading producers. Other significant crops include cassava, yams, and maize. The fishing and forestry sectors also contribute notably..

Natural resources

Ghana is rich in natural resources such as gold, oil, and bauxite. Gold mining is a major contributor to the economy, with the country ranking as one of the top gold producers in the world. Oil production began in earnest in 2010, and the Jubilee oil field, in particular, has significantly bolstered the economy. However, commodity price fluctuations often impact revenues.

Recent Developments

The Ghanaian economy, like many others, was significantly affected by the COVID-19 pandemic, which caused disruptions in trade, tourism, and public health costs. Recovery efforts are ongoing, with renewed focus on fiscal discipline and sustainable growth. The government’s "Ghana Beyond Aid" agenda seeks to reduce dependency on foreign assistance by fostering local innovation, industrialization, and sustainable development.
